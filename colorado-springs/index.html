Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Colorado Springs

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of the year.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A capacitor that's starting to fail costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected for a season, can take out the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement. Comfort Challenges Colorado Springs Homeowners Face

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and humidity and airborne grit slowly foul outdoor condenser coils, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Dirty filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

We also see the full range of component failures in local homes: weak capacitors that leave the condenser humming without starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are far cheaper to address early. If your system is doing anything unusual, strange noises, weak airflow, rooms that won't hold temperature, bills creeping upward, a quick diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes a costly breakdown.
